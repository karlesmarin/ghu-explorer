An instrument for gauge‑Higgs unification, and the papers it comes from

Pick a model of extra‑dimensional matter and this page computes what it predicts: which Wilson‑line domain is legal, where the vacuum sits, the Higgs mass, the anomaly bill, and how high the compactification scale can go. It runs entirely in your browser, offline, with nothing to install — and every number it prints says what is known about it.

I read the paper and want to try it → The instrument. One bulk model, eleven computations over it, the paper's own model preloaded. I found the tool and want to know what it is → The series. Seven papers on six‑dimensional gauge‑Higgs unification, each with its record, its status, and what has moved since.
Read this before quoting a number from here. Our α does not reproduce the published α of N. Haba, T. Yamashita, JHEP 05 (2004) 059 (hep-ph/0401185), eq. (3.20), and the disagreement is not a constant — so it is not a convention that could be absorbed. Every absolute scale on this site (TeV, GeV) inherits that open question and is not citable until it closes. What escapes it entirely, because no normalisation enters them: the mass ratio, the bill in eighths, and the two arithmetic laws. The instrument states this in its own header and carries a permanent “what this cannot tell you” panel; the reasoning is in the log.

Every number carries what is known about it

Four words, and they are fields in the exported result card rather than decoration. This is the whole difference between an instrument and a demonstration.

wordwhat it promises
theorem Proved, in a paper of the series, and the page names which one.
verified Checked against an independent computation — usually brute force over the same object — but not proved.
measured Computed here, and inheriting whatever open questions its inputs carry. Every absolute scale on this site is of this kind.
unknown Said out loud, with the reason, instead of a plausible number. The tool refuses to fill a gap it cannot compute.

The fourth is the one that costs something, so it is worth saying why it is there. This project has retracted one of its own novelty claims before — a closed‑form identity published in May 2026 and withdrawn in July on finding it was a corollary of Helset–Martin–Trott 2020. The algebra was right; the claim about its standing was not. A tool that could not label its own output unknown would have no way to do that to itself.

What has moved

The papers are frozen; this site is not. Every change is logged with what moved, why, and whether it affects the version of record.

2026-08-29extensionPart VIII · affects the record: no

Part VIII enters the instrument — the map backwards, the clusters, and the census

what

Part VII maximises 1/R₅ over the lattice of bulk contents and publishes a ceiling. Part VIII runs the same map backwards: given a target box in the two observables the closed form predicts, it returns a bulk content or a named certificate that none exists. The instrument had the ceiling and none of the inverse. It has both now, in two sections that sit next to Hierarchy because they are the same map read the other way.

Design a scale does the deciding. Type a scale — or click the axis — and the page walks the rungs, issuing one of five certificates where nothing sits: floor (identity (II) asking for an A₄ below the empty content, which non-negative multiplicities forbid), cone, congruence, dual (an exact rational Farkas pair) and exhaustion (the rung is finite and was enumerated). Two more things are reported and are explicitly not certificates: rung, which excludes the rung and not the target, and budget, which means the enumeration was stopped. A design is only a design once the exact potential agrees that the electroweak point is the deepest place on (0, 1] — W > 0 compares the two symmetric points and nothing else.

Count a rung does the counting. A dynamic programme over the two partial moments gives N(A₄, 8D) for every A₄ at once, in about twenty milliseconds, where the enumerator that built the same contents one by one took twenty-five minutes and ran out of budget below 5 TeV. The four rung totals — 69 022 464 contents — land exactly on what that enumerator built, by an algorithm that shares nothing with it. The section also proves and checks the recurrence N(A₄, 8D+6) = N(A₄, 8D) − P, which is why the four counting curves superpose, and reads the fibre of the measured-mass point with Part VII's completeness theorem: 81 contents there share all five coordinates, so they are not 81 models that agree — they are 81 ways to build one potential, one of 19 multiplets and one of 198.

why

A ceiling is a bound; the inverse direction reports a set, and the set is not what the bound suggests. The clusters the instrument now draws are finite point sets — rung one is 35 values of 1/R₅, about 31.5 GeV apart — and between the rung-three and rung-one clusters there are 2682 GeV with nothing in them, forty-five times the widest gap inside either cluster. A finite set is disconnected for free; a macroscopic stretch closed by an exact rational certificate is not.

Nothing in the record moves: this is Part VIII arriving in the instrument, not a correction to anything already deposited.

so

Nothing to redo, and three defects paid for on the way — all of them in this page's own code, all now guarded. The first drawing painted each cluster as a bar, which is the misreading the paper warns against; it is a comb now, computed here. The point sweep then hit a budget cap on rung three and printed 54 points as though they were 65, with no mention of the cut — the cap is derived from the same threshold that chose the rung, and a deliberately capped sweep is now part of the harness. And the fibre panel picked its class by size, which on this rung gives 86 and not the paper's 81: two classes there have a single 2W, and the one the paper means is the one at the measured Higgs mass. Each class is one potential, so it has one scale, and that is what selects it.

check
open Design a scale, press resolve the clusters into their points, and read the note: rung 8D = 1 is 35 points and not an interval, 31.46 GeV apart on average, widest interior gap 59.44 GeV — so the certified gap is 45× that, computed on the page. Then type 7.5 into the target and press design it: floor, one rung, a proved no-go. Then 9.0: a content of eight multiplets at 9008 GeV, verified on the exact potential. In Count a rung, press build the table: the four rung totals land on the enumerator's own 69 022 464 contents, counted by a dynamic programme in about twenty milliseconds.
2026-08-27notePart VII · affects the record: no

A verdict can have no subject — the true-vacuum flag was a boolean where three answers were needed

what

A second outside audit read the corrected source and found the correction's own edge. The verdict had become true: symmetricOK && deepest !== false — right whenever there was something to test, and wrong whenever there was not. deepest is null on every content that never reaches the minimiser, and in this language null !== false is true. So a content with no electroweak breaking and W > 0 exported alpha_min: no electroweak breaking next to vacuum.true: true. The auditor built it from this page's own coordinates: 2 × 7(+,+), where the gauge seed gives 8D = −27, 2W = −3 and each 7(+,+) adds 8D = −6, 2W = +2, so 8D = −39 < 0 and 2W = +1 > 0. Reproduced here to every digit, and the Python engine of Part VII now produces that row into tests/reference_models.json so a reader can check the null against an implementation that shares no line of code with the page. The screen was never wrong — it said "But D ≤ 0, so there is no interior minimum for it to be the vacuum of" — but the screen is not what a third party reads. vacuum.true is now one of {true, false, null} beside a named state: true-vacuum, false-vacuum, no-electroweak-breaking, no-branch-located, undetermined. Null is not a verdict, and it no longer pretends to be one.

The same audit asked for the globality half to stop resting on a positional tolerance — it decided the branch was the deepest point if |α_global − α_closed| < 0.02 or the gap in F was negligible. The closed form is an expansion, accurate to 0.71 % under the largest α of their Table 1 and to 20 % out at α = 0.229, so a fixed window in α is a guess about how wide a basin is. It is gone. The closed form now only locates the basin: localMin walks downhill from it to the minimum it is about, numericMin finds the deepest point with no bracket at all, and the verdict is F against F at two numerically refined minima. On their five rows the two points coincide to better than 10⁻⁶; on the 2026-08-26 counterexample they are 0.0839 and 0.5660, and F is lower at the second by 1.07.

why

The first is the honesty floor read at the wrong layer. The instrument had learned that a necessary condition is not a sufficient one, wrote the two halves, and then joined them with an operator that cannot tell "no" from "not asked" — a three-valued question answered in a two-valued type. The second is the same instinct as the first correction, applied one level deeper: a tolerance in α is a claim about the potential's shape, made where a comparison of two computed depths was available for the same cost.

so

No printed number moves and the frozen record is untouched: every published row has a branch, and on all five the refined branch minimum is the global one. What moves is what the instrument exports about contents that break nothing — and the standard the globality half is held to. Both are pinned: _test_hierarchy.mjs fails on the old boolean and on a localMin that returns its input, and the shipped tests/run.mjs now checks that a reference row with no electroweak point makes the page claim neither vacuum. Fired both on purpose before believing them.

check
load 2 × 7(+,+) in the hierarchy section — 8D = −39 < 0 with 2W = +1 > 0, so there is no electroweak breaking at all; the exported card now reads state: no-electroweak-breaking and true: null where it used to read true: true. tests/run.mjs, which ships with the page, pins it against the Python engine, and the globality half no longer rests on any positional tolerance

The code

The instrument is one self-contained HTML file with no external request in it, built from a Python and JavaScript source tree by build/build_app.py, which re-runs every harness and refuses to publish a red build. Each released paper also gets a frozen copy of the tool as it stood at publication — see editions — so a link in a paper keeps working even after the living instrument has been rewritten.
