What this tool is for

Use this to add explicit matter at the two orbifold endpoints and inspect two consequences: changes to the anomaly ledger and possible boundary masses for unwanted zero modes. The local groups at the two endpoints can differ, so a representation that is available at one boundary need not be available at the other.

First steps

  1. Edit the model in SU(N) builder — or press the example to load Kawamura's SU(5).
  2. Read the two branes first: they are different groups whenever the orbifold breaks anything.
  3. Add fields with the + buttons, or pick a massless mode and let the partners panel say which representations contain its conjugate.
  4. Press solve to get the local charges that cancel the linear channels.

Reading the results

Two verdicts, kept apart on purpose: the bill before and after, and the massless count before and after. The same field can pay one and not the other, because the charge that cancels an anomaly is not in general the charge a mass term needs. The surviving count is a lower bound — the rank test assumes generic couplings.

Worked example

Check the cost of removing a zero mode

  1. Load Kawamura's SU(5) example.
  2. Choose an unwanted massless mode and inspect its possible partners.
  3. Add a suggested local representation.
  4. Compare both the anomaly ledger and the boundary-mass condition, including its charge.

What to expect: A field that helps one requirement can leave another unresolved. Read the two outcomes independently.

Scope and limitations

  • Solving the linear anomaly channels does not by itself solve every cubic condition.
  • Generic-coupling rank information does not specify actual numerical masses or a complete Yukawa sector.
  • Only the supported local representations and boundary-mass conditions are examined.

If something is unclear

The anomaly-solving charge does not permit the desired mass term.

Keep both conditions explicit and try another supported completion; do not treat the two charge requirements as interchangeable.

A representation introduces additional fields.

Read its full local decomposition, then include those fields in the updated ledger and surviving-content count.
