What this tool is for

Use this to avoid counting different matter descriptions as different one-loop potentials. It also demonstrates a common limitation of approximations: two models can agree on the few coefficients used near a symmetric point while differing elsewhere. The algebraic comparison and the numerical graph are deliberately shown together so you can tell an identity from a visually small difference.

First steps

  1. Use the shared model as content A and copy it into content B.
  2. Apply a displayed kernel relation or edit B's multiplicities.
  3. Read the five-coordinate comparison as well as the curves.
  4. Load the equal-moment example to see how matching local moments can leave different full potentials.

Reading the results

ResultMeaning
Five potential coordinatesThe complete identity test for this stated family. Equality has a stronger meaning than agreement at sampled plot points.
Two potential curvesA numerical illustration of the algebraic comparison. Use the full range to inspect structure hidden by a local zoom.
Moment diagnostics and evidenceShow which selected moments coincide and what the available exact or interval checks establish about the example.

For the stated potential family, equality of the five coordinates is the algebraic equality criterion. Two overlapping plots alone cannot prove an identity. Matching only the selected local moments is weaker and can leave different global behaviour.

Worked example

Compare exact equality with approximate agreement

  1. Copy A to B and verify coordinate equality.
  2. Apply a permitted kernel relation; the content changes while the identity criterion remains satisfied.
  3. Load the equal-moment example.
  4. Compare the local and full plots and read the separate full-potential conclusion.

What to expect: A kernel rewrite and a low-order moment match have different logical strength. Use the coordinate or certificate statement, not the apparent thickness of an overlaid curve.

Scope and limitations

  • The equality criterion concerns the implemented one-loop potential family and its conventions. It does not prove that all particle spectra, couplings or boundary sectors are identical.
  • Numerical agreement at finitely many phase values does not establish an analytic identity.
  • A canonical representative of a potential is not automatically a canonical representative of the complete physical theory.

If something is unclear

A relation arrow cannot be used.

Check whether the proposed rewrite would require a negative multiplicity.

The curves look identical but the tool distinguishes them.

Read the exact coordinate comparison and use the full phase range; a plot may hide a small or distant difference.
